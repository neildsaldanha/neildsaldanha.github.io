Case studies

The problem. Growth outreach to customers was typed by hand, one message at a time. It took a whole day to reach about 200 customers, and there was no budget for paid tools.

What I did. I found a free way with an Excel VBA macro. It takes a column of names and numbers, fills them into a pre-written message, and sends each one when I press Start. Later I automated most day-to-day customer service with WhatsApp Business flows (Zepic, 360dialog) and a Claude project for inbound email.

Result. Over 750 customers reached in under 4 hours instead of 200 in a full day, with no extra headcount or software cost. The rest of my day was free for other work.

The problem. Logging one delivery complaint took 20 to 25 minutes of looking up details across different systems.

What I did. I built an Excel tool that fills in the customer, order, payment and courier details from a phone number or email, and a macro that posts each issue to Slack.

Result. About 5 minutes per complaint, a cut of 75 to 80%.

The problem. While I was trying to win back inactive customers (people who signed up but never ordered), many told me other brands had better Trustpilot scores. They had joined to look at the menu, not to buy. With only about 30 reviews, we were short on social proof.

What I did. I took over review generation and built the outreach step by step. I contacted every customer we delivered to each week, and sent the Trustpilot link to the ones who replied and had a good experience.

Result. About 250 reviews in six months, roughly eight times where we started.

The problem. Price came up again and again as a main reason customers cancelled their subscription.

What I did. I piloted plans where customers pay upfront for future weeks, for example 12 or 24 weeks, in return for a small discount on the total. I also ran smaller discounted plans, and asked these customers to refer people they knew to similar plans.

Result. Over £25,000 in sales. Customers committed for longer, and referrals brought in more prepaid customers.

The problem. The kitchen and operations teams needed a regular, clear view of what customers thought of the food.

What I did. I survey customers every week with Microsoft Forms and turn the results into a report that opens the weekly team call, with action points for kitchen and operations. The old report only scored taste, texture and portion, which didn't tell the chef what to change. I rebuilt it to break every dish down by flavour, carbs, protein and sauce, and portion.

Result. When a trend shows up, the team makes a plan in the same call and the change goes into the next production cycle. My LinkedIn post about it
