Case studies

Click a project to see the problem, what I did and the result.

ProblemGrowth outreach to customers was typed by hand, one message at a time. It took a whole day to reach about 200 customers, and there was no budget for paid tools.
What I didFound a free way: an Excel VBA macro. It takes a column of names and numbers, fills them into a pre-written message, and sends each one automatically at the click of a Start button. Later I automated most day-to-day customer service with WhatsApp Business flows (Zepic, 360dialog) and a Claude project for inbound email.
ResultOver 750 customers reached in under 4 hours instead of 200 in a full day, with no extra headcount or software cost, which freed the rest of my day for other work.
ProblemLogging one delivery complaint took 20-25 minutes of looking up details across systems.
What I didBuilt an Excel tool that fills in customer, order, payment and courier details from a phone number or email, plus a macro that posts each issue to Slack.
ResultAbout 5 minutes per complaint, a 75-80% cut.
ProblemWhile trying to win back inactive customers (people who signed up but never ordered), many told me other brands had better Trustpilot scores. They had joined to browse the menu, not to buy. We had only around 30 reviews, so we were missing social proof.
What I didTook ownership of review generation and built the outreach step by step: I contacted every customer we delivered to each week, and sent the Trustpilot link to those who replied and had a good experience.
ResultAbout 250 reviews in 6 months, roughly 8x.
ProblemPrice came up again and again as a main reason customers cancelled their subscription.
What I didPiloted plans where customers pay upfront for future weeks (for example 12 or 24 weeks) in return for a small discount on the total, plus smaller discounted plans. I also encouraged these customers to refer people in their network to similar plans.
ResultOver £25,000 in sales, customers committed for longer, and referrals brought in more prepaid customers.
ProblemThe kitchen and operations teams needed a regular, clear view of what customers thought of the food.
What I didI survey customers every week with Microsoft Forms and turn the results into a report that opens the weekly team call, with action points for kitchen and operations. The old report only scored taste, texture and portion, which didn't tell the chef what to change, so I rebuilt it to break every dish down by flavour, carbs, protein and sauce, and portion.
ResultTrends get a plan straight away and changes go into the next production cycle. Read my LinkedIn post about it.
